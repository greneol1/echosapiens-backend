
Charles Darwin (1809–1882) was an English naturalist, geologist, and biologist best known for developing the theory of evolution by natural selection. His work transformed biology and profoundly influenced modern science.

Major concepts associated with Charles Darwin:

1. Natural Selection
Darwin proposed that organisms with traits better adapted to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce. Over generations, these advantageous traits become more common within populations.

2. Common Descent
Darwin argued that all living organisms share common ancestors and that life diversified gradually over immense periods of time.

3. Adaptation
Species evolve through the accumulation of beneficial variations that improve survival and reproduction in specific environments.

4. Evolutionary Competition
Darwin described a “struggle for existence” in which organisms compete for resources, mates, and survival opportunities.

5. The Origin of Species
Published in 1859, “On the Origin of Species” introduced the theory of evolution by natural selection and remains one of the foundational works of biology.

6. Human Evolution
In “The Descent of Man,” Darwin applied evolutionary theory to humanity, proposing that humans and other animals share common ancestry.

Key books:
- On the Origin of Species
- The Descent of Man
- The Voyage of the Beagle
